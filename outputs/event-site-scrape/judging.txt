How the finals are judged.

All five finalist projects already work. The final score is about how well each team used Codex, chose what to build, and understood the problem.

Eligibility gates.

These determine who reaches the final round. Once a project passes, judges begin from the same starting line. Functionality is a gate—not a scored bonus.

01

Clean start

Ideas and teams may exist beforehand. Code, prototypes, design artifacts, and project work begin on Saturday.

02

Runs live

The project itself runs during review. A video, slide deck, or mockup cannot stand in for the working build.

03

Claims have evidence

If a feature or Codex contribution cannot be shown when asked, judges score as though that claim was not made.

What judges score

Three questions. Thirty points.

Each criterion is worth the same amount. Breadth, polish, and ambition matter only when they produce a better, more thoughtful build.

01

Codex Craft

Did the team use Codex deeply, appropriately, and demonstrably, not merely as one-shot code generation?

Look forSpecific artifacts, sessions, decisions, steering, recovery from failure, and visible payoff. Breadth counts only when it served the work.

10

Exceptional

Codex materially shaped several meaningful parts of the build. The team shows strong evidence, explains how it steered the work, and chose capabilities because they fit the task.

8

Above the bar

Codex carried substantial work across the build, including purposeful use beyond routine generation. Concrete evidence shows a clear payoff.

7

Solid finalist

Codex contributed meaningfully, but mostly through routine or one-shot use. Evidence is limited or the team’s steering is difficult to see.

5

Not demonstrated

Codex use is thin, incidental, or only asserted. The team cannot point to a specific contribution and its visible result.

Examples: planning, research, coding, debugging, testing, review, terminal work, parallel agents, and visual or multimodal input.

02

Product Judgment

Did the team choose the right scope and turn it into one coherent, complete core experience?

Look forA clear job, deliberate priorities, sensible boundaries, and cuts that protect the core experience instead of scattering effort.

10

Exceptional

Exceptionally focused and coherent. The team made excellent cuts and completed the experience that matters most.

8

Above the bar

A complete, well-chosen core experience with sensible boundaries. Secondary work supports rather than distracts from it.

7

Solid finalist

A usable core exists, but scope or priorities feel somewhat scattered. Several choices compete for attention.

5

Weak

The pieces exist, but they do not yet form a coherent product experience. The team built around the core instead of completing it.

03

Problem & Insight

Is the project grounded in a specific problem and audience, with a thoughtful or non-obvious approach?

Look forA named audience, evidence or firsthand knowledge, a solution aimed at the cause of the pain, and choices that are right, not merely different.

10

Exceptional

A specific audience and pain are backed by evidence or firsthand knowledge. The solution addresses the cause, with a non-obvious choice the team can defend.

8

Above the bar

A real problem, plausible audience, and solution that clearly addresses it. The project’s distinct angle survives questioning.

7

Solid finalist

The problem is broad or generic. The solution is reasonable but interchangeable, and answers about the user stay abstract.

5

Not demonstrated

The problem is asserted rather than grounded. It feels like technology looking for a use, with no clear answer to who needs it and why.

The scale

Seven is honorable.

A 7 means solid finalist work. An 8 means clearly above the finalist bar. A 10 is exceptional and should be rare. A 5 means the criterion was weak or not demonstrated.

1.  01
    Watch and ask.
    Every team gets the same core question: “Show us one thing Codex did today that materially improved this project.”
2.  02
    Score provisionally.
    Each judge records 10, 8, 7, or 5 for all three criteria immediately after the pitch.
3.  03
    Calibrate once.
    After all five teams, judges may revise their own scores against the written anchors. They do not negotiate a shared score.

Score the work and the judgment behind it.Do not reward presentation charisma, polished slides, team size, prior experience, huge scope, feature count, or trendy technology by itself.

Tie-break

Codex Craft first.

Higher combined Codex Craft score, then more 10s across all judge scores, then more 8s. If still tied, use the head judge’s ranking recorded before totals are revealed.

Remember

Proof beats claims.

If a feature or Codex contribution cannot be shown when asked, judges score as though that claim was not made.
